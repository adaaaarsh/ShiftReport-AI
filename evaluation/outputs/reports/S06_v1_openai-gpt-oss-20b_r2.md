### 1. Summary of Events  
- **Issue:** At approximately 3:00 PM, the quality inspection scanner on Line C rejected 14 consecutive good parts.  
- **Immediate Action:** Re‑calibrated the scanner using the master part.  
- **Outcome:** Rejection rate returned to normal levels.  
- **Verification:** Re‑ran the 14 previously rejected parts; all passed inspection.

### 2. Root Cause Analysis  
- The scanner’s calibration drift caused false positives.  
- No external factors (e.g., sensor contamination, power fluctuation) were observed.

### 3. Corrective Actions Taken  
- Re‑calibrated scanner with master part.  
- Re‑inspected the affected batch; all parts passed.  
- Logged the incident in the quality control system.

### 4. Preventive Measures  
- **Schedule:** Implement a daily calibration check at the start of each shift.  
- **Monitoring:** Add a real‑time alert for rejection rates exceeding 5% over a 10‑part window.  
- **Documentation:** Update SOP to include a “quick calibration” step if a sudden spike in rejections occurs.

### 5. Follow‑Up Items  
- **Maintenance Team:** Verify sensor alignment and firmware version.  
- **Quality Team:** Review calibration logs for the past 30 days to ensure no similar patterns.  
- **Training:** Brief operators on the new calibration procedure and alert thresholds.

### 6. Additional Notes  
- No impact on production volume or downstream processes.  
- All parts that were initially rejected have been re‑tested and cleared for shipment.
